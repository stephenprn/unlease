# Changelog

All notable changes to Unlease are written here: the server and every app share one version. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/2.0.0/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Until 1.0.0, a minor version (0.2.0) may break things: each break is marked **Breaking:** with what to do about it, and [docs/UPGRADING.md](docs/UPGRADING.md) says how to upgrade.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Clock**, a fifth app: an alarm clock for Android, its alarms also shown and edited in its web app (`/clock`). Set an alarm for once or for some days of the week, with a label, one of nine sounds (gentle, classic or lively, all freely licensed; or one of your phone's own) and vibration. As it rings, the screen lights over the lock screen with the time, the weather where you are and your day from Agenda, with "View the day" to open it in Agenda; snooze with one tap, stop by sliding the pebble along its track (or with the notification's buttons, or the volume keys if you choose). The evening before (21:00 by default), Clock warns you when tomorrow's first event starts before your alarm or less than 30 minutes after it (both adjustable), and offers to ring earlier that morning. Each alarm rings on the minute through Android's alarm-clock alarms, even with the battery saver on or the app closed, after a restart before the phone is unlocked (in a plain window of its own, since the app waits for the unlock), and again after an update; during a call it rings at an eighth of its volume, and fully once the call ends; an alarm volume left at nothing is raised to a third for the ring; a ring cut short (the app closed by Android while it rang) starts again within its ringing time; a ring missed while the phone was off rings late if you are still within its ringing time, or is said missed. The home warns when something would keep an alarm from ringing (notifications, the ringing notification turned down, Do Not Disturb letting no alarm through, the alarm volume, exact alarms, full-screen notifications, the battery's « Restricted »). An alarm can be for an Agenda event ("Wake me up for this" on the event): it rings 30 minutes to 2 hours before it, as you choose, and follows the event if it moves. A repeating alarm can stay quiet on public holidays and your days off ("Not on holidays and days off", from Agenda), and "Weekdays" sets the days you work. An assistant's « set an alarm for 7 » sets it at once; another app's « set an alarm » opens the form, filled. Administration counts your alarms. Install `clock-vX.Y.Z.apk` from the release beside the others; turn Clock on in Administration › Apps. The weather comes from Open-Meteo through your server, asked with your phone's approximate location (rounded to about a kilometre) or a place you choose in Settings.
- In Clock on Android, swipe the morning panel sideways to go to the previous or next day of the week. When the day has an event still to come, a button beside it opens a new alarm already set to the event's time and title, repeating on the same days when the event repeats weekly or daily (once otherwise).

- On Android, Contacts and Agenda can copy what they hold into the phone's own address book and calendar: turn on "Copy to the phone's contacts" or "Copy to the phone's calendar" in Settings › On this phone. Your contacts then show in the phone's dialer, Messages and WhatsApp, which name who calls or writes, and your events in the phone's calendar, where the Clock, Maps and a watch see them, each source (your own events, each calendar you follow, the holidays) as a calendar you can hide there. The copy is read-only for the other apps and follows every change, with the app closed too; "Open in Contacts" on a copied person opens them in the app. It lives under an account of its own ("Unlease"), so your Google or other accounts are never read nor touched, and turning it off, or removing that account in Android's settings, takes the copy away. Notes and labels are not copied, and Agenda's reminders still ring from Agenda only.
- Immich joins the Administration ("Photos on Immich", also offered during setup when Pages or Contacts run): link your Immich with its address and an API key (with `album.read`, `asset.read`, `asset.view` and `person.read`), which your server tries before keeping it and never sends to your devices. In Pages, on the web and on Android, "Photos from Immich" in the `/` menu shows one of your albums (its latest photos, and how many more) or photos you pick among the latest or by what they show (Immich's smart search); a photo opens large, and the block links to Immich. In Contacts, "From Immich…" in a contact's photo menu takes their photo from the faces Immich recognised, those named like the contact first; the server keeps a copy, so the photo stays even if Immich goes. The backups' Markdown links an Immich block to its album or photos.

- Pages keeps a history of each page, on the web and on Android: "Version history" in a page's menu lists the versions your server kept, by day, with what each changed; open one to read it as it was, each block that changed since or is no longer on the page marked, and "Restore this version" brings it back ("Undo" on the message that follows takes it back). Your server keeps a version a few minutes after you stop editing a page: every one for a day, then one per day for 90 days. A restore keeps the page's sub-pages, its place and whether Claude may read it. Versions stay on your server: they are not in its backups, and they need the server (an app kept on this phone or in this browser alone has none). The pictures and files a version holds are kept as long as it is.

- In Carnet, a collection can be shown four ways, on the web and on Android, kept per collection on each device: **List** (a row per link, as before), **Gallery** (the pictures large and alike, to compare at a glance), **Mosaic** (the pictures at their own proportions, like a mood board, with their prices on a tag) and **Table** (a line per link with its site, price, the date it was saved, your note, and a column for each detail Claude reads on most of its links, such as the brand, the colour or the director; the price and date headers sort it). On the web the layouts are the four icons over the links; on Android the icon beside the collection's menu.

- Agenda can sum up your day each morning in one notification, on Android and in the browser: in Settings, turn on "Morning summary" and pick its time (from 5:30 to 10:00). It counts the day in its title (events, a birthday by name, reminders, pages, and Carnet's reminders you missed) and lists it in order, a few lines with their times; a tap opens "Today". A day with nothing stays quiet unless you turn on "Even on days with nothing". On Android it is armed on the phone, quietly (no sound), and kept up to date as your events, notes, contacts and pages change; in the browser your server sends it, as it sends reminders, and needs your browser's notifications allowed. A phone off or a server stopped at that time still gets it later in the morning, once.

- Agenda subscribes to calendars that need a login, over CalDAV: your mailbox's calendar (an Open-Xchange messagerie, such as La Suite numérique's), Nextcloud, Fastmail, iCloud and others. In "Other calendars", paste the calendar's address, or just the server's or your account's: when it asks for a login, the form asks for your login and password (an app password if your account offers them), and when the login opens several calendars, which one to follow. The password is kept by your server alone, never synced to your devices nor written in its backups (after a restore, the calendar asks for it again); it is sent only over `https://`, to that server alone. When it changes, "Change the login" on the calendar's row takes the new one.

- In Pages on the web, pages in the sidebar can be chosen together, as in a file manager: Shift+click selects every page between the one open (or the last clicked) and the one clicked, Ctrl+click (⌘+click on a Mac) adds or removes one, and Shift+↑/↓, Space and Ctrl+A do the same from the keyboard. Dragging one of them moves them all, into a page or between two; a right click (or the menu key) duplicates them, moves them to another page or puts them in the bin in one step, with one "Undo". Ctrl+Shift+↑/↓ moves the pages among their siblings, Backspace bins them, Ctrl+D duplicates them, * opens every page at that level, and Esc or a click elsewhere lets them go.
- Pages' editor on the web gets more of Notion's keys: Shift+click selects every block from the selection (or the block you are typing in) to the one clicked, Ctrl+click (⌘+click) adds or removes a block; selected blocks are dragged from anywhere on them, and a click on one puts the caret there. ⌘⌥0 to 9 (Ctrl+Shift+0 to 9 elsewhere) turn a block, or the selected ones, into text, a heading, a to-do, a list, a toggle, code or a page; Ctrl+/ opens the block's menu (so does a right click on the selection); Ctrl+Enter ticks the selected to-dos or opens the selected toggles; Home and End reach the first and last block; Ctrl+Alt+T opens or closes every toggle of the page. The shortcuts dialog (?) lists them all.

- Agenda's Administration has "Your week" (formerly "Public holidays"), on the web and on Android: tick the days you work (Monday to Friday until you change them), for every device. Agenda's "Working days only" setting (formerly "Hide weekends") then leaves the other days out of the week and the month, and Clock's "Workdays" rings on them.
- Agenda can mark an all-day event as a "Day off", on the web and on Android: Clock's alarms that ask for it stay quiet on the days it covers, as on public holidays. Its page says so, its bar wears a sun on the web and on Android, and Android's schedule says "Day off" for its time.
- "Wake me up for this" on a timed Agenda event still to come, on the web and on Android, opens Clock's new alarm linked to that event, whenever Clock can be opened (turned on on your server, or installed on the phone).

- Contacts can clean up your address book, on the web and on Android: in Settings › Data, "Clean up contacts" gathers tools that each show every change before it is made, as before → after, ticked when safe: **Duplicates** (as before), **Country codes** (numbers written without theirs, 06 12 34 56 78, written with the code of the country you pick, +33 6 12 34 56 78; those whose length doesn't fit that country are left unticked), **Number formatting** (dots, 0033, no spaces written one way; the same number twice in a contact kept once), **Names** (capitals or lower case, extra spaces, an e-mail or a number in place of a name, a whole name in the first name offered for a split), **E-mail addresses** (capitals, spaces, `mailto:`, a known typo in the domain, the same address twice; one that isn't an address opens the contact's form) and **Empty contacts** (no name, number or e-mail, to the bin). "Fix" writes what is ticked at once, and the slip's Undo takes it all back; "Ignore" sets a doubtful one aside on that device.

### Changed

An event's colour picker says "Default colour" when it has no label to take one from.

- Pages' Notion import keeps the pages' emoji icons: export your workspace from Notion a second time as HTML and drop both archives together (only the icons are read from the HTML one). - In Pages on the web, selected blocks wear their wash alone, without the edge around each one nor the "+" and the grip beside every block; a page's "…" and "+" in the sidebar no longer sit on a box of flat colour: the title fades out under them.
- English is now the source language of the code (every sentence the interface shows is written in English, with its French in the dictionaries) and the server's messages are sent in English, which the apps show in your language. Nothing changes in what you see; an app older than its server shows those messages in English until it is updated.

- Headers no longer blur or fade what scrolls under them, in every app on the web and on Android: the top bar, the search strip, a sheet's or a dialog's head and Agenda's day headings show a thin line once content goes under them, and none at rest; a footer keeps its line. Fades remain only where content continues sideways (chip rows, strips, wide tables) and at the cut ends of a short list inside a menu or a box. On a phone-sized web window the bottom bar lost its shadow and shows the same line while the page runs on under it.

## [0.1.2] - 2026-11-12

### Added

- Every app's import is now a step-by-step wizard, on the web and on Android: where the data comes from and how to get it, then a preview of everything the file holds with what will become of each item (new, completed, already here, or left out and why), filtered and searchable, then the choices that need you, each with its default and its cost, then the import and a report of what came in and what was left out. Nothing is written before "Import". It opens from Settings › Data, and the first run now offers it as an optional step ("Your data", skipped with "Later") while the app holds nothing yet.
- Pages imports a Notion export ("Markdown & CSV" `.zip`) or Markdown files and folders (Obsidian…), as nested pages with their pictures, files, callouts, toggles, tasks, tables and links between them; Notion's databases become tables. The pages go under a new page, at the top of the tree or inside a page you pick, optionally kept out of Claude's reach, and "Undo" takes the whole import back. On Android, Pages reads `.md` files; a Notion `.zip` is imported from the web.
- Carnet's Keep import lets you untick notes one by one, and choose whether Keep's archived notes stay archived, go on the board or stay in Keep.
- Contacts' import lets you leave people out one by one, choose which of Google's labels to keep, whether to complete the contacts you have, take the photos and keep Google's favourites; a person read twice, or with nothing to know them by, says so; "Undo" takes the import back, leaving alone what you changed since.
- Agenda imports several `.ics` files at once, or Google's `.zip` export on the web; it can keep only the events to come or those of the last year, file each calendar under a label of its name, and take the file's reminders, none or your own; a series already imported takes the dates the file changed since.
- "Erase data" at the foot of Settings › Data, in every app, on the web and on Android: tick the parts to erase (in Agenda your own events, imported events, subscriptions and labels apart; in Carnet notes, collections, the links in Uncategorised, labels and the facts Claude notes; in Contacts people, favourites and labels; in Pages your pages and the journal), then type your server's token again and the word DELETE ("SUPPRIMER" in French). When your server backs up to S3, a backup runs first and must succeed. What is erased leaves every device and the server, without going through the bin.
- In Carnet, "Convert to collection" in a list's menu, on the web and on Android: each item's link becomes one of the collection's links, the words beside it its comment, a ticked item a done link, and the list goes to the bin ("Undo" brings it back). If some items are not links, it says which.
- In Carnet, the addresses written in a note or a list can be opened, on the web and on Android: on Android they show in link colour, and putting the caret on one shows it above the note's bar, to open with a tap (an address in a ticked item opens at a tap). On the web the same row appears, and Ctrl+click (⌘+click on a Mac) on an address opens it in a new tab.
- On Android, every app now syncs with the app closed: what you wrote offline goes to your server soon after you leave the app and is retried with a growing wait until the server takes it, a few seconds after any network comes up (your VPN included, for a server reached only at home or through a VPN), and every 15 minutes while a network is there; photos and files go up too, and the reminders, birthdays and events that came in are armed. When Android holds it back (a battery set to "Restricted", or Data Saver on mobile data), the app says so when you open it and in Settings under "Background sync" and in Administration › Server, with a button that opens the right page of Android's settings and what to choose there.
- In Agenda, "Import a calendar" is one tap away: in the navigation on the web (over the bin) and in the drawer on Android, and "Other calendars" offers "Import an .ics file" beside subscribing by address (a file's events are copied once; an address keeps being followed). Settings › Data still has it, and on the web an `.ics` dropped anywhere on Agenda still opens it.
- In Agenda, on the web and on Android, an import can be taken back: "Undo" on the message that follows it removes everything it added, and picking the same `.ics` file again later offers "Remove these events", which moves the events it brought in to the bin (asked first, even those changed since, and with its own "Undo").
- On the web, restoring a page from Pages' bin offers "See the page" beside "Undo" on the message that follows; Agenda's bin offers "See the event" and Contacts' "See the contact" the same way.

### Changed

- Carnet's Keep import turns a note that is mostly links into a collection too (a list of 50 links with one line of plain text no longer stays a note): each line without a link is kept as the comment of the link above it, dropped, or replaced by a link you paste, line by line in the wizard.
- On the web, Contacts' and Agenda's import pages moved to Settings › Import (`/contacts/settings/import`, `/agenda/settings/import`); their former addresses lead there.
- In Agenda, importing a file brings back its events that are in the bin, instead of counting them as already there.
- On Android, what is pinned at the foot of a screen or a sheet (a form's buttons, the note editor's bar, Pages' block bar, a question's field) now meets what scrolls above it with a clear line, instead of the content fading out above it.
- On Android, Contacts' letters stand in a column at the list's left, beside each letter's first person: the letter stays at the top while its people scroll, then leaves with the last of them. It no longer flickers over the rows or cuts the panels while you scroll.
- Building an Android app from source (`npm run android`, or `assembleDevRelease` for a release-like build) now makes a separate dev app, "Carnet Dev" and so on, with a striped icon. It installs beside the app you use instead of replacing it, never reads its connection, and doesn't answer its links. Connect it to a server of your own.
- In Carnet's "Facts Claude notes", a collection's two choices are now called "Adaptive facts" (Claude picks them by each link's kind) and "Custom facts" (the ones you choose). Each custom fact has a bin button at its row's end to remove it at once, with "Undo" on the message that follows.
- The sync status, in every app, says why the server can't be reached, how many changes wait and that they are safe on this phone or in this browser, and when the last sync got through, even after the app restarts; photos and files not yet uploaded count as waiting. A browser tab hidden with changes waiting keeps trying every minute and as soon as the network comes back, and an app in front tries again the moment a network or a VPN comes up instead of waiting out its retry.
- Checkboxes look like checkboxes in every app, on the web and on Android: a crisp box that fills once ticked, with the tick cut through so the note's colour shows in it, instead of a small outlined icon. A done item (a checklist's, a to-do, an ingredient cooked) fills in grey and steps back with its words; a choice (what to import, erase or share) fills in ink.

### Fixed

- Carnet no longer takes numbers ("96.8", "2.7"), file names ("photo.jpg") or names typed without a space ("M.Dupont") for web addresses: a Keep note listing them is imported as a note again, not offered as a collection of links. An address written without `https://` must now look like a site (`zara.com/fr`, `www.example.org`, `monsite.studio`).
- In Agenda's small months (the navigation's calendar and the year view on the web, the year view on Android), the dot under a busy day no longer touches its number: on Android it no longer covers it.
- On the web, the message at the foot of the window no longer slides sideways when a dialog or a menu opens and the scrollbar goes away.

## [0.1.1] - 2026-11-11

### Added

- On Android, every app's icon holds shortcuts on a long press: a new note, list or link and the search in Carnet; a new page and the search in Pages; a new event, an event in a few words, today and the search in Agenda; a new contact, the favourites, the birthdays and the search in Contacts.
- On Android, text selected in any app can go to Carnet ("Note in Carnet": a link is filed, words become a note) or become an event ("Create event": Agenda's quick add, already filled in).
- On Android, "Add to calendar" and "Add contact" in other apps (a booking in your e-mail, a number in your dialer or Messages) open Agenda's or Contacts' new form, filled in; nothing is saved until you save it.
- On Android, a `webcal://` link opens Agenda's subscriptions with its address, ready to subscribe, and a link that opens the calendar at a moment (from the Clock or another app) opens that day.
- On Android, Quick Settings tiles for a new note (Carnet) and a new event (Agenda), and Pages in the share sheet: shared text becomes a new page.
- On Android, pull Agenda's views down to sync, as in the other apps.
- On the web, `C` makes a page in Pages, as it makes a note, an event or a contact in the other apps (`P` still works); the `?` help lists Agenda's and Contacts' own keys; `E` opens the selected event's form in Agenda and `#` bins the selected contacts in Contacts, as their buttons said.
- Carnet's starter collections (Fashion, Films & series, Restaurants, To read, Home, Travel) come with their own facts for Claude to note, chosen as they are made; one made earlier starts from them when you give it its own.

The line you are typing on always stays clear of it, as when writing on the last line of a note or above a form's buttons. On Android, tapping into a long note or page no longer jumps it to its top before scrolling back to your line: it stays put, then glides just enough to keep the caret above the keyboard. Dialogs on the web no longer draw a line under their title or over their buttons.

- Lists move the same way in every app, on the web and on Android: what you remove fades where it was and the rest close ranks; what you add settles in (contacts, bins, labels, Pages' home and tree, Agenda's reminders).
- On Android, Back from Archives, Reminders, the bin or a label in Carnet goes back to your notes instead of leaving the app.
- On Android, swipes follow one rule: a row that acts (a link marked done, a contact called) acts only when you let go past its threshold, which clicks; what goes away (a note archived, a week turned) goes when flung too, with a click.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-10

The first release: the Unlease server and four apps, Carnet, Pages, Contacts and Agenda, on the web and on Android.

### Added

- The Unlease server, one Docker image for the whole suite (`ghcr.io/stephenprn/unlease`, amd64 and arm64): offline-first sync for every app, photos and files, a daily copy of its database, backups to any S3 storage as a readable archive (Markdown, JSON, vCard and iCalendar), and files kept in a Seafile library if you want them there.
- One setup for the whole server, done by the first device: which apps it runs, then Claude, S3, Seafile, Seerr and the public holidays for the apps chosen. The Administration, in every app, changes them later; an app turned off keeps its data.
- **Carnet**, a Google Keep of your own: notes, checklists and drawings Keep's way (labels, colours and papers, reminders, photos, undo), moved in from a Google Keep export; collections of links Claude reads, summarises and files, noting what you choose for each kind of link, with modules on the links (price tracking, promo codes, a recipe scaled with its shopping list, a series' recap, the way to a place, a copy kept as a page, a question to Claude).
- **Pages**, a Notion-like knowledge base: nested pages with headings, tasks, toggles, callouts, tables with typed columns, columns, pictures, files and drawings, templates for a new page, Carnet's notes, collections and links cited or embedded, and Claude answering questions about your pages.
- **Contacts**, a Google Contacts of your own: names, numbers, e-mails, addresses, birthdays, labels and favourites, imported from Google Contacts (vCard or CSV) or from the phone; duplicates found and merged; a call, a message or WhatsApp in one tap; birthday notifications and wishes; gift ideas kept in Carnet.
- **Agenda**, a Google Calendar of your own: day, three days, week, month, year, a schedule and « Aujourd'hui »; repeating events, reminders, time zones, labels, places with their weather and guests from Contacts; subscribed calendars, public holidays, Google Calendar's export imported and your own published at a secret address; an event typed in a few words, and Claude asked about your week. Your contacts' birthdays, Carnet's reminders and the days written in your pages show beside your events.
- The apps' data linked: Pages and Carnet mention contacts and events (type `@`), each lists where it is mentioned, an event's notes become a page in one tap, and a link can be kept as a page.
- On Android, an APK per app (`carnet`, `pages`, `contacts` and `agenda`), all signed with one key: the second app installed reuses the first one's connection and colours. Home-screen widgets in Carnet, Contacts and Agenda, Carnet's share sheet, and notifications that ring on time.
- On the web, each app is its own installable web app under its path (`/notes`, `/pages`, `/contacts`, `/agenda`), served by the server, with a launcher between them, keyboard shortcuts, a search that also finds the settings, and Agenda's reminders as push notifications.
- Each app's look: light or dark, a primary and an accent colour, a ground and the paper's grain, given to the other apps in one tap; the same look for Seafile, Seerr and Immich (« Habiller vos autres services… »).
- French and English.
- A demo on GitHub Pages (https://stephenprn.github.io/unlease/): the four web apps with made-up notes, pages, people and events and a server simulated in the page, so nothing leaves the browser. Each release updates it.
