# Architecture

Unlease is a suite of apps that replace Google's, on a server you run: stop leasing your data to Google, and unleash yourself. Four apps are ready, **Carnet** (id `notes`: notes, checklists, drawings, collections of links), **Pages** (id `pages`: nested pages, a wiki), **Contacts** (id `contacts`: an address book) and **Agenda** (id `calendar`: a calendar). Below, `core/x`, `design/x`, `notes/x`, `contacts/x`, `calendar/x` and `pages/x` are `packages/<name>/src/x`.

## What the suite shares, and what each app owns

| Shared by the suite | Owned by each app |
| --- | --- |
| One server and one Docker image; the sync, files, backups, the drive, the Administration | Its record types, its routes and background work on the server (`server/src/apps/<app>`) |
| One setup (the server's apps, Claude, S3, Seafile), done once by the first device | Its screens: a web app (`web/<app>`, on the shared shell `web/shared`), an APK (`mobile/<app>`); its mark |
| One look (the design system, `packages/design`) and each device's appearance | Its English (`packages/<app>/src/i18n/en`) |
| Data: one store of records on the server, references from one app into another | What it lets others show of its records (its package's models and services); its own search and spotlight |

## One repository, apps installed one by one

Everything is in one repository, because the apps share the sync, the design system and data that points from one app into another: a change to any of them is one commit, tested against every app at once, and nothing is published between them. Proton keeps Mail, Calendar and Drive's web apps in one repository for the same reasons ([their engineering blog](https://proton.me/blog/engineering-polyrepo-monorepo)); Ente keeps its photos and authenticator apps and their server together. Folders follow the toolchains:

| Where | What |
| --- | --- |
| `packages/core` | The platform: records and their merge, the sync protocol and engine, ids, references, rich text, the dictionary machinery, formatting, the search engine, the apps' registry (`apps.ts`, `appWords.ts`), the setup's steps and words (`services/setup.ts`), the server's settings (`sync/settings.ts`). |
| `packages/design` | The design system's data, the same for every app: palette, appearance, continuous corners, the pebble, outlines, Onde's glyphs, emblems, collages, the marks (Carnet's card and leaf, Pages' sheet, the Administration's server, Seafile's folder), Signika's advance widths and line wrapping. |
| `packages/notes` | Carnet's domain: its record types (`sync/types.ts`), models, services, the contracts of its server routes, its English. |
| `packages/contacts` | Contacts' domain: the `contact` and `contactLabel` record types, names and their order, phone numbers (to call, to compare, WhatsApp's international form), birthdays, addresses, the search, vCard and Google's CSV read and written, the import's plan, its English. |
| `packages/calendar` | Agenda's domain: the `calendarEvent`, `calendarLabel` and `calendarFeed` record types, days and times in a zone, repeat rules (RRULE) and their occurrences, the views' layout, iCalendar read and written, subscriptions and holidays as records, the edits of a repeating event, birthdays and reminders from the other apps, quick add, search, questions, its English. |
| `packages/pages` | Pages' domain: the `block` record type, blocks, page text and search, tables, the embeds of Carnet's collections, questions about pages, copies of articles, its English. |
| `web` | A web app per app on one shell: `shared` (the shell: setup, Réglages, the Administration, storage, sync, the router, components), `notes` (Carnet's web app), `pages` (Pages' web app, which shows Carnet's data with Carnet's own pieces, `@notes/*`), `demo` (the demo and its fake server). One install, one lint, a build per app ([web/README.md](../web/README.md)). |
| `mobile/shared` | What every Android app shares: storage, sync, the setup, the Administration, components, theme, the native specs and the shared Kotlin. No install of its own. |
| `mobile/calendar` | Agenda's Android app: its views, its reminders as notifications, an `.ics` opened or shared, its widgets. |
| `mobile/contacts` | Contacts' Android app: its screens, the phone's own contacts read for an import, calls and WhatsApp, its widgets. |
| `mobile/notes`, `mobile/pages` | Carnet's and Pages' Android apps, each its own install and its own APK. Pages shows Carnet's collections and links with Carnet's own pieces, borrowed through `@carnet/*` (its projection, the link row, the drawing editor) and nothing else: Carnet's stores never load in Pages' process ([mobile/README.md](../mobile/README.md)). |
| `server` | One server for the suite: the platform, and each app's module in `src/apps/<app>` (listed in `src/apps/registry.ts`). |

The packages are TypeScript sources with no dependency, compiled into every program (Hermes, the browser, Node) under one name each, `@unlease/<package>/…` (path aliases in each program's tsconfig, bundler and test runner). Their order is enforced by lint: `core <- design <- notes, contacts <- calendar <- pages`. An app's package sits after the apps whose data it shows: Agenda shows contacts' birthdays and Carnet's reminders, so it may read Contacts' and Carnet's packages; Pages shows Carnet's collections, notes and links and mentions contacts and events, so it may read all three; Carnet's and Contacts' read no other app's (Carnet names the events and people its notes mention through what its programs hand it, `noteMentions`). The root is an npm workspace for the web app and the server (one lockfile; the image installs only what they use); React Native pins its own React, ESLint, Jest and Babel, so each phone app keeps its own install, and Metro resolves what the packages' and `mobile/shared`'s files import from the app's own `node_modules`.

### Adding an app

1. Its entry in `core/apps.ts` (`status: 'soon'` while it is announced: the setup and « Applications » show it as « Bientôt disponible »), its words in `core/appWords.ts`, its references in `core/refs.ts`.
2. Its package, `packages/<app>` (record types with `defineRecordTypes`, its English), slotted into the lint's order after the apps it reads.
3. Its server module, `server/src/apps/<app>` (a `ServerApp`), listed in `server/src/apps/registry.ts`.
4. Its web app, `web/<app>` (an `index.html`, an entry installing its module on `web/shared`), built by `web/scripts/build.mjs` (with its icons and manifest in `web/build/brand.ts`) and served under its path by the server (`WEB_APPS` in `server/src/http/web.ts`); its mark in `design/mark.ts`; its APK, `mobile/<app>`, on `mobile/shared` ([mobile/README.md](../mobile/README.md)), added to the release's matrix and to `tools/release/version.mjs`.
5. `status: 'ready'`: servers can turn it on.

## Data across apps

- **Record types belong to apps** (`core/sync/schema.ts`): each app declares its own with `defineRecordTypes` (Carnet's `note`, `check`, `link`, `label`, `attachment`, `linkTemplate` in `notes/sync/types.ts`; Pages' `block` in `pages/sync/types.ts`; Contacts' `contact` and `contactLabel` in `contacts/sync/types.ts`; Agenda's `calendarEvent`, `calendarLabel` and `calendarFeed` in `calendar/sync/types.ts`): each field's check, the long text fields merged instead of overwritten (with their limit, and a merge of their own for drawings), the defaults a rollback writes back, the records every device seeds. Names are unique across the suite, so one store holds every app's records (the server's `records` table, an app's SQLite table on the phone or IndexedDB database on the web), and a type tells which app a record is (`appOfType`).
- **A client syncs what it shows** (`SyncRequest.types`): the types of its apps and of those they read (`typesOfApps(appsShownBy(app))`): Carnet's phone app Carnet's, Contacts' and Agenda's types (its notes mention people and events), Contacts' Contacts', Pages' its own, Carnet's, Contacts' and Agenda's, Agenda's its own, Contacts', Carnet's and Pages' (birthdays, reminders, the days written in pages, the names of what it mentions), on the web as on the phone. A device keeps the list beside its cursor, with a fingerprint of what its build knows of those types (`schemaFingerprint`: each field and the values its check accepts), and pulls again from the start when either changes: a build that learns a field or a value an older one dropped from what it pulled gets it back.
- **Ids are UUIDv7** (`core/services/ids.ts`, RFC 9562): minted offline on any device, unique across every app, sorted by creation (a 48-bit millisecond time, a counter within the millisecond, random bits), and what iCalendar and vCard expect of a UID (RFC 7986 recommends a UUID). Records every device seeds keep fixed ids (`inbox`, the starter collections), so two devices make the same one.
- **References** (`core/refs.ts`): a type and an id. `REF_TYPES` lists what people can point at in the whole suite, each with its app and its record type: `note` (a collection is a note) and `link` for Carnet, `page` (a `block` of kind `page`) for Pages, `contact` for Contacts, `event` for Agenda (a repeating event as a whole); another app's things join it. A reference sits in rich text as a mention `{@type=id}` (`core/services/richText.ts`, the suite's text format), in a block's `targetType`/`targetId` (bookmarks, embeds), or in a field (a link's `copy`, the page made of it). A type a build does not know, written by a newer client, travels and stays as it is and shows as unavailable, so an older phone never destroys what a newer app wrote. A reference into an app the device does not have opens elsewhere: a phone app opens the other app (`carnet://…`, `unlease-pages://…`, `unlease-contacts://…`, `unlease-agenda://…`) when it is installed, else its web app; a web app opens the other web app's page (`suitePlace`: `/notes/note/<id>`, `/notes/link/<id>`, `/pages/<id>`, `/contacts/contact/<id>`, `/agenda/event/<id>`).
- **Work across apps goes through the server**: Carnet's « Garder une copie » asks Pages' module (`POST /api/pages/copies`), which makes the page under the server's clock and sets the link's `copy`, so Carnet's clients need no page types; Agenda's « Créer une page de notes » and « Note du jour » ask Pages' module too (`POST /api/pages/from-event`, `POST /api/pages/daily`); Carnet's « Ajouter à l'agenda » asks Agenda's (`POST /api/calendar/from-link`) for an event drawn from the link, then opens Agenda's form with it, so nothing is written before the person saves.
- **The apps' registry** (`core/apps.ts`): every app, installed or not, with its id, name, slug (its databases, its browser storage), scheme (its phone links), status and the apps it reads. `/api/health` lists the apps a server runs.

## The server's apps

The platform (`server/src/` outside `apps/`) runs the same for every app: the token, the sync, files and their display copies, settings, the Administration, backups to S3, the Seafile drive, the daily database copy. Each app adds its part as a `ServerApp` (`server/src/apps/types.ts`), listed in `server/src/apps/registry.ts`; the platform never imports an app's package.

| Hook | Carnet's (`apps/notes`) | Pages' (`apps/pages`) | Contacts' (`apps/contacts`) | Agenda's (`apps/calendar`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `routes` | Sorting by Claude, the link modules, Seerr, Claude's spending, the notes mentioning something, gift ideas kept from Contacts | Questions about pages, copies of articles, a meeting's notes and the day's note, the pages mentioning something | A photo taken from an address (`POST /api/contacts/photos`, Google's exports) | The events mentioning something, a subscription read again, the countries and regions of holidays, the weather, an event read by Claude (typed words, a saved link), questions, Web Push subscriptions |
| `publicRoutes` | | | | The published calendar (`/ical/<secret>.ics`): mounted before the token's check, it carries its own secret |
| `stored`, `start`, `stop` | The reading of new links, resumed at start, its queue drained at stop | | | A subscription added or changed is read; every 6 hours each is read again; the reminders' push ticker |
| `chores` | Its bin emptied after a week | Its bin emptied after a week | Its bin emptied after a week | Its bin emptied after a week; a new year's holidays |
| `filesInUse` | Photos and link pictures | Files and pictures blocks point at | The contacts' photos | The files joined to events |
| `files`, `markdown`, `readme` | Its files under `Carnet/`, its notes and collections as Markdown | Its files under `Pages/`, its pages as Markdown | Its photos under `Contacts/Photos/`, everyone as `Contacts.vcf` and `Contacts.md` | Its files under `Agenda/<day title>/`, its own events as `Agenda.ics` (any calendar imports it) and `Agenda.md` |

**Apps on and off** (`ServerSettings.apps`): the server runs the apps its Administration turned on (every ready app on a new server, then what the setup chose). An app turned off answers its routes with 404, stops its background work and its chores; its records still sync, are stored and backed up, so turning it on again finds everything. **The server's setup** (`ServerSettings.setup`) is done once: the first device sets it up (the apps, then Claude, S3, Seafile, Seerr), every other device only connects.

Backups hold every app's records in one `unlease.json` (`unlease-2026-10-26T03-00-00Z.tar.gz`), each app's Markdown and files under its own folder.

## Data and sync

- **Records:** every note, checklist item, link, label and photo is a record: a map of fields, each with the hybrid logical clock (`core/sync/hlc.ts`) of its last write.
- **Merge:** field by field, the larger clock wins (`core/sync/record.ts`). Commutative and idempotent, so devices converge whatever the order. Two offline edits to the same field keep the later one, except typed text and the lists a type merges as sets (an event's days taken out: `MergedField.set`, what either side added stays, what either side removed goes; a client or a server without it keeps the later list).
- **Drawings** (a note of kind `drawing`, a page's `drawing` block) keep their strokes in a `drawing` field as text, one stroke per line (`notes/services/drawing.ts`: an id that sorts by time, the tool, size and ink, the points in units of a sheet 1000 wide, polyline-encoded). Strokes are only drawn or erased, never changed, so the field is merged as a set, not as words: everything either side drew stays, everything either side erased goes, in time order (`mergeStrokes`, chosen by `mergeField`). It travels as typed text otherwise (bases, `saveTypedText`, `saveBlockText(…, 'drawing')`). The sheet is as tall as what is drawn on it (`drawingHeight`), so a card or a block keeps its proportions without measuring.
- **Formatted notes:** a text note's `body` is plain text until its first mark or heading; then the note is `rich` for good and its body is lines, each a page's rich-text markup after an optional `# `, `## ` or `### ` (`notes/services/noteBody.ts`, saved by `saveNoteLines`, merged word by word as typed text). Everything but the editors reads a body through `bodyText` (the words: search, widgets, reminders, the clipboard) or `bodyMarkdown` (a backup).
- **Typed text:** a note's title and body, a checklist line and a link's comment (`MERGED_TEXT_FIELDS` in `core/sync/textMerge.ts`) are merged word by word when two devices change them before seeing each other's edit: edits in different places both survive; where both rewrote the same words, both versions are kept, the older first, as alternatives on one line (« mardi / jeudi ») or one after the other when they span lines. Rewrites a few shared words apart in one sentence count as one passage, so each version stays whole instead of interleaved word by word. A device keeps, beside each unacknowledged edit of such a field, the value it started from (its base, `core/sync/bases.ts`) and sends it with the push; the server merges a concurrent edit into one text under a fresh clock of its own; a device merges an edit that reaches it before its own is acknowledged; an editor merges what lands while someone types (`saveTypedText` in both apps' actions). A device's own earlier write is never merged with (its answer was lost: last writer wins), and when what the server holds descends from a version a device sent (a merge, or another device's edit on top of it), that version is the base of the device's next push (`MergeLineage`, kept in the database's `lineage` table in the push's transaction, so a restart in between forgets nothing: merging from the older base would type that device's words twice). Texts too far apart to align by words merge by lines; a long passage both rewrote keeps the side that changed more.
- **Protocol:** one endpoint, `POST /api/sync` (`core/sync/protocol.ts`): the device pushes pending records (with the bases of their typed text) and receives everything after its cursor (the server's own sequence). With nothing to send, the request waits up to 25 s for news (long-poll), so a link read on the server or an edit on the web appears within a second.
- **Loop:** `core/sync/engine.ts`, shared. Pushes are paged by count and UTF-8 size; the server's answer is acknowledged before it is merged; a record edited during a request stays pending (acknowledgement compares clocks). A phone sent to the background or a hidden tab stops long-polling, but first sends what was just written. Offline, both retry with a growing wait, which ends the moment the network is back (the browser's `online`, Android's validated default network or any network coming up, a VPN's included, through the `Connectivity` module; a long-poll held over the network that was there before is asked again over the new one) or when the server names one (`Retry-After`). A hidden tab with changes unsent tries again every minute and at the browser's `online`. The phone syncs with the app closed too (*Sync with the app closed*, under Phone specifics). A change stays pending, on the device, until the server has answered for it: nothing is dropped while the server is out of reach, however long. The sync status says what waits, that it is safe on the device, and when a sync last got through (kept across starts: `lastSyncAt` beside the cursor); on the phone, files not yet uploaded count as waiting (`SyncTask.pending`).
- **Lost data:** the server names its dataset (`epoch`, in every answer); each daily snapshot gets a name of its own, so restoring one always counts as a new dataset. A device that has seen more than the server holds (another kind of backup restored, the data wiped) also makes it choose a new name: each start of the server is a run with a random id (`runs` in `meta`); every answer names the server's last sequence and its run (`head`, `run`), the device keeps them as one value and sends them back, so a head from a run the server never had, or outside its run, gives a restore away whoever syncs first, however far behind the device was, and whatever the dates say. The phone then also uploads its photos again (one the server has is answered at once); the web keeps no copy of a photo once uploaded. Every device that knew the old one, or was moved to another server, sends everything it holds again.
- **Clocks:** no single date is trusted, the server's included (a box without a battery-backed clock boots days behind until it gets the time). A device more than a minute behind the server writes on the server's time; it never follows the server backwards. Every request carries the device's time. The server replaces a pushed clock more than a day ahead of the latest of what the other devices last said (moved on by the time since), the clocks they stored and its own date: a phone with the wrong date. Until another device has spoken since the server started, the clocks the others stored stand in for their word, so the first device to sync after a restart is caught too; a server where no other device ever wrote replaces nothing. Each source can only raise that bar, so a device or a server that is behind never gets the right clocks replaced. The replacing clock is the server's, at the time the others vouch for and after what the server held of that field (that device wrote it last, and shows it). The device then takes back the server's clock for that same value (`adoptReplacedClocks`, any tab of the browser), so the field takes later edits. Every server write (a merge, a link read, the bin's tombstones, a rollback, a repair) is stamped after the clocks of the fields it overwrites (`ServerClock.stampOver`), whatever the server's date: a write under a clock a device already holds would stay on the server and nowhere else. The bin counts its 7 days from the clock of the trashing write, not the device's `trashedAt`. A device more than 2 minutes off the server says so in its sync status (`clockNote`).
- **Phone storage:** records are JSON documents in one SQLite table (`mobile/shared/src/data/db/migrations/001_initial.ts`, every phone app's, bases since `002_bases.ts`, every record type since `003_every_type.ts`: a type the table refused made the records pulled with it skipped), read into memory at boot. This departs from the house rule of typed columns on purpose: the merge works on field maps, nothing aggregates in SQL, and a field written by a newer client must survive a round trip through an older one. The server stores records the same way, commits with `synchronous = FULL` (a device stops resending a change once told it is stored) and writes a consistent copy of its database every day (`backups/`).
- **Ordering:** fractional index keys (`core/services/fractionalIndex.ts`): moving one item rewrites one record. Two devices adding at the same place mint the same key (ties sort by id); an insert or a move between tied neighbours also gives the later twin a new key in the same batch (`placeAt`), and `positionBetween` never throws on them. That new key is a write like any other: a twin dragged elsewhere at the same time ends where the later of the two writes put it. Inserting again and again at one place lengthens keys (a character every six inserts or so, and the schema refuses one past 200): once a new key would pass `RESPACE_AT` (64), `placeAt` gives the run of siblings around the gap short keys again, in the same batch.
- **Deletion:** a `deletedAt` tombstone, never a removed row. The bin empties after 7 days (server job); a note a device restores after the bin emptied comes back with what the purge took (its photos are kept 30 days for that), unless a person deleted it for good; so do a page with what it held, a contact, and an event with its exceptions (`server/src/sync/revive.ts`: only the purge's tombstones, which carry the server's clock, are lifted).
- **Newer and older:** a field or a value a program does not know is dropped where it arrives, never coerced (`sanitizeRecord`). A device dropping one from what it pulls gets it back once updated (the fingerprint beside its cursor). A server dropping one from a push does not tell the device, which counts it as stored: that field's write is lost for good, so the server ships before the apps that write a new field, type or value, and a phone app is updated after the server, never before.

## Imports and erasing

- **Every import is one wizard** (`core/services/importFlow.ts`, drawn by `web/shared/src/ui/features/importing` and `mobile/shared/src/ui/features/importing`): where the data comes from and how to get it, a preview of every item with what becomes of it (new, completed, already here, left out and why; filtered and searched), the choices that need a person (each with its default and its cost), then one batch of records and the files after it, counted, and a report of what came in and what was left out. Nothing is written before « Importer ». Each app's package reads the file and plans the records (Carnet's Keep archive, Contacts' vCard and Google CSV or the phone's own book, Agenda's `.ics`, Pages' Notion export and Markdown); each app hands its wizard to the shell (`AppModule.importer` on the web, the phone's `Import` screen and its setup's `importer`). Réglages › Données opens it (`/<app>/settings/import` on the web), and the first run offers it as a step of its own while the app holds nothing yet, on the server or on a device keeping everything to itself (`setupImportShown`), skipped with « Plus tard ».
- **« Effacer des données »** (`core/services/erase.ts`; each app's parts in `<app>/services/erase.ts`): an app's records erased for good, in parts the person ticks (Agenda's own events but not its subscriptions…), behind guards: the server's token typed again and checked by the server (`checkServer` with what was typed), the word « SUPPRIMER » ("DELETE") typed in capitals, and, when the server backs up to S3, a backup that runs first and must succeed. What waits on the device is sent first; then the tombstones go as one batch, as « Vider la corbeille » writes them, under the device's clock, so the sync carries them everywhere and no revival lifts them. What every device seeds (« Non classé ») is never erased; subscriptions' and holidays' events are the server's, erased by removing the subscription.

## Link reading

1. A link saved from the share sheet or the quick input lands in "Non classé" with `status: 'pending'` and `autoFile: true`.
2. The server fetches the page (SSRF guard, a link-preview user agent when blocked, oEmbed for video and music sites), extracts meta tags, JSON-LD and text, and asks Claude once for a structured result: title, the site's name as people write it, kind, summary, price, key facts, best image, and the collection it belongs in. A page the server cannot read, Claude fetches itself (`web_fetch`); its pictures then come from what that fetch returned.
3. It keeps up to three images as WebP blobs, then writes everything in one change; the phone's long-poll returns and the transfer panel shows where the link went. With no server, or a server without a key, links are kept plain (`skipped`) from the start: nothing waits on a reading that cannot happen.

4. **Sorting saved links** (`POST /api/sort`, `notes/sync/sorting.ts`): the app sends links as their reading left them (no page is read again) and the collections with their sentence and newest titles (`sortTargets`, from which the server files a new link too). The server asks Claude in batches of 60 (3 at a time) and answers moves by id; after a failed batch, or once the budget is spent, no new batch starts and the moves of those that answered come back marked `incomplete`. The app writes them as one undoable batch, skipping a link moved meanwhile, and runs one sort at a time. `file` puts a link where it clearly belongs or leaves it ("Ranger Non classé", a new collection taking from Non classé, the links of collections deleted from their page or a board selection); `review` lists what no longer belongs in a collection, for the person to keep or untick. A call counts toward the monthly budget, not toward the links read (`analyses.task`).
5. **Who filed it:** a link's `filedBy` is `claude` when Claude chose its collection, `person` when the person did (moved it, added it to a collection, kept a review's move, sent a deleted collection's links to another), null when it landed in Non classé by default. "Ranger Non classé" leaves the person's choices alone. A link shared again within 30 days, its first copy deleted since, is filed from cache into the same collection by id, renamed or not.
6. **Once per collection** (`notes/services/savedLinks.ts`, `addLinks` in both apps): an address a collection holds already (trackers aside) is not added to it again; one another collection holds is added with that link's reading copied (`ready`, the same image blobs, its `readWith`), so the page is not read twice, when both collections read with the same template (the same one of the person's own, or both by kind; otherwise it is read again with its own). Filed by Claude, an address any live collection holds is not added at all (it would land beside itself). A slip says which.
7. **Templates** (« Ce que Claude relève » in the apps, `notes/models/templates.ts`, `notes/services/templates.ts`): a link's facts (`attributes`) are the fields of the template it is read with, each a name (the fact's label), a type (shown as the answer's shape, « un nombre ») and what to put in it. Its collection's own list first (`note.templateId`, a `linkTemplate` with `kind` null, named after the collection; never « Non classé »), else its kind's usual facts: the preset (`KIND_TEMPLATES`, the facts Claude always looked for), or the person's change of it, kept under the kind's fixed id `template-<kind>` (two devices write one record; set back, it is deleted and the preset reads again). The fixed system prompt lists every kind's preset, French / English, written from `KIND_TEMPLATES` through `presetNames` when the module loads, so it stays cached and never drifts from the presets; the person's own fields go in the message only (`promptFieldLines`: the name, the type's shape in fixed English, the hint): « Fill exactly these fields » for a collection's template, the kinds the person changed as `<kinds>` otherwise, and when Claude files a link, each list with a template carries its fields in `<lists>` (a link filed there fills those). Claude's facts are matched back to the fields by name (a field born of a preset also by that preset's name in the other language), labelled with the field's name exactly, in its order, eight at most (six for a preset, as before). Every ready write carries `link.readWith`, the version it was read with (`readingSignature`: « preset », or a hash of the fields' names, types and hints); null is a reading from before templates, read with the presets. The reading cache is keyed by it too: a reading kept is reused only when it was read with the version the new link would be read with now, so a changed template is never answered from an older reading. The apps show it from the collection: its page « Ce que Claude relève » picks Claude's usual facts or its own list, edited there and saved in one batch (`saveCollectionFacts`: its template made, changed, or deleted when it goes back to the usual facts and no other collection notes with it); each kind's usual facts are changed from the overview. A client compares `readWith` with the facts now (`linksReadBefore`) to offer « Relire » and « Tout relire »; reading again is always the person's choice. The preview (« Aperçu sur un lien », `POST /api/notes/template-try`, `notes/sync/templateTry.ts`, `server/src/apps/notes/analysis/templateTry.ts`) reads one page as a link is read (the same digest and prompt, the fields as a collection's) and answers what Claude put in each field by its id, writing nothing: billed in the month's budget (`analyses.task = 'template'`), kept a day by address, language and version (as is a link already read with those very fields: both answer for nothing), the page itself fetched once in ten minutes, 30 tries an hour that ask Claude.
8. **Link modules** (`notes/models/modules.ts`, `notes/services/modules.ts`): small tools a collection shows on its links, picked per collection in the synced note field `modules` (null keeps `DEFAULT_MODULES`; unknown keys from a newer client pass through). A module shows on a link when the collection picked it, it fits the link (`moduleFits`: its kind, a price, an address, not stamped done) and what it needs is there (`moduleWorks`: nothing, a server, Claude, or Seerr linked on the server). Both apps put them in one row of small buttons under the link's title (`ModuleBar`) and manage them from the collection's menu ("Modules"). Promo codes (`POST /api/coupons`) and offers elsewhere (`POST /api/offers`, market `new` or `used`) are Claude with `web_search` (5) and `web_fetch` (2), basic versions on Haiku, no structured format (web search always cites, and citations cannot go with one), the JSON read from the text after the last tool block (`server/src/apps/notes/analysis/searches.ts`): kept six hours by task, normalized address and language, a running search joined, a search outlives the app hanging up and stops at 90 s, billed at $0.01 a search on top of the tokens, a call cut off billed at a whole search's estimate. Ratings (`POST /api/ratings`, kept a week), a series' recap up to the episode the person stopped at (`POST /api/recap`, kept a month under the address marked with the stop, `#s2e5`, so each stop has its own answer) and other outlets on an article's story (`POST /api/coverage`, kept half a day) go through the same `LinkSearches`, each with its own prompt (`prompt.ts`). A question about a link (`POST /api/ask`, `questions.ts`) sends Claude the page's words (its article whole when one stands out, up to 30 000 characters), with web search only when the person ticks it (3 searches); nothing is kept, the same question at once is joined, and a page gone for good (a 404) is never paid for unless the web may answer. A recipe's ingredients and method (`POST /api/recipe`, `recipe.ts`) come from its structured data (`recipeIngredient`, `recipeInstructions` as text, steps or sections), free, else from Claude reading the page; the cook mode asks for the steps (`steps: true`), so a page whose data lists ingredients but no method costs Claude once, then it is kept a month, a method Claude found missing included (`stepsRead`); one reading runs per address (the list joins any, the cook mode one that looks for the method, else asks again once it ends), the read and Claude share one 85 s deadline, a page gone for good is never sent to Claude, and the cook mode falls back on the ingredients kept when the page cannot give more; a method written as one block is cut into steps before each « Étape n », else by sentences. Seerr (`POST /api/seerr`, `server/src/apps/notes/seerr.ts`) is the server calling the address the Administration gave, with a plain fetch (it is often on the local network) that never follows a redirect, its key sent only once the address has answered `/api/v1/status` as Seerr (dropping the key tries nothing): with the key it searches TMDB's titles there and picks the link's kind and year, without it the answer is Seerr's own search; the apps remember Seerr's address with the server's settings, ask once every few minutes per link, and open Seerr's search if it does not answer. "Pour N personnes" and the cook mode's timers live in each app's memory (`kitchen.ts`: a factor per recipe, scaled in core by `scaleIngredient`; durations found by `stepTimers`); the web keeps the screen on with the Wake Lock API and chimes a rung timer, the phone keeps the screen on natively and schedules each timer as a notification. "Garder une copie" (`POST /api/copies`, `article.ts`, `copies.ts`) is the server alone: the page's main content as block drafts, its pictures kept as `link` blobs; the app makes the page (`createPageFrom`, one batch), the link's bookmark card first, which is how it finds the copy again (`copyOf`). "Y aller" is a Google Maps directions address built in core (`directionsUrl`); "Liste de courses" writes a checklist in one batch (`addToChecklist`). Each module's runs are rows of `analyses` under its own `task` (`ModuleTask`: `recipe` for both recipe modules; migration 7 dropped the CHECK so a new module needs no table copy).

The Claude key, model and monthly budget are server settings changed from the apps (`GET/PUT /api/settings`). Without a key, links are kept as plain links (`skipped`); the settings screens offer to read them once a key is set.

## Administration: backups and files

The server's settings live in the Administration, apart from the device's Réglages: the server's address and token, the apps it runs, Claude, the S3 backup and the drive, then each app's own (Carnet's Seerr, Pages' questions). The wording of their statuses is shared (`core/services/admin.ts`); the routes and statuses are `core/sync/admin.ts`.

**The Administration is in every app**, under « Réglages » at the navigation's foot, with its own navigation and its own pages. Its home lists its sections, each with its state in one line (`appsLine`, `serverLine`, `claudeLine`, `backupLine`, `driveLine`; an app's own line comes with its section: `seerrLine`, `pagesAskLine`); each section is a page (web `/<app>/admin/<id>`, phone the `Admin` screen with its `section`), its « how it works » at its head. The sections every app shares come first, in `SHARED_ADMIN_SECTIONS` (their ids are addresses: never renamed); the app brings its own, shown under its name: on the web `AppModule.adminSections` (id, title, glyph, line, body), on the phone the `appSections` its `Admin` screen hands the shared one. The shell lists them (`web/shared/src/ui/features/admin/places.ts`) and never imports an app; an app's sections are set from that app. This is how suites that share one server do it: Nextcloud's administration is one settings app where each app registers its own section beside the built-in ones ([Nextcloud](https://docs.nextcloud.com/server/stable/developer_manual/digging_deeper/settings.html)); Odoo's Settings app has a block per installed app, under that app's icon, after the general settings; Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 put their admin console beside the apps, for whoever may administer. Unlease has one token and no roles, so whoever holds it sees the Administration; a role, if one comes, hides its entry and its routes, not the sections one by one.

- **Backups to S3** (`server/src/backup/`): on a schedule (`6h`, `daily` by default, `weekly`) or on demand, a readable archive (`unlease.json` with every record of every app and its clocks, each app's Markdown under its folder, for Carnet every note, collection and page, `LISEZMOI.md`) and each photo and file once (`fichiers/<id>.<ext>`, an upload's original only), except those Seafile holds: files live there only, and `unlease.json` says where. A small SigV4 client, path-style, trying again what S3 could not answer; the archive is tar.gz, streamed both ways. Each run lists what S3 holds, so a file lost there is sent again. The newest snapshots this server made are kept, never another server's; a file goes once no live record points at it and no kept snapshot names it. Nothing is backed up while the server holds nothing (a new server about to be restored), nor during a restore. Settings are tried before they are kept (list, write, delete).
- **Restoring** an archive from S3 or a downloaded one (a full archive holds the files): `merge` keeps each record's own clocks, so only what the server lacks comes back; `rollback` writes the backup's values under fresh server clocks, so they win on every device, and keeps what was created since. A server holding nothing but "Non classé" always merges; its devices then send back what is newer. A file comes back when what a backup keeps of it (the original) is missing, even if a display copy is here: from the archive, from Seafile where the backup says it is (nothing copied or sent again), else from S3.
- **First run** (`core/services/setup.ts`, both apps' `SetupScreen`): one decision (the server's address and token, or the notes kept on this device), the device's colours and ground, then, only on a server that held nothing when the device came, « Votre serveur »: the Administration's own sections as rows, each optional and opened from its row: a restore first (the newest snapshot chosen, always a merge; notes from S3, files from Seafile), Claude, S3 (starting from Scaleway Paris), Seafile, Seerr. A device joining a server already in use goes from its colours straight to the board.
- **Durability:** blob files are flushed to the disk before they are renamed into place and their row committed; the phone's SQLite commits with `synchronous = FULL`; the web asks the browser to keep its storage (`navigator.storage.persist`).
- **Files in a drive** (Seafile, `server/src/drive/`): originals of photos and page files live in a folder of one library (`/Unlease` by default), under each app's own folder and named after what holds them (Carnet's notes and pages: `server/src/apps/notes/files.ts`, also the archives' layout); renames move them, the collector's removals delete them. The server keeps the display copies and streams an original from Seafile when asked; a file already in place (a server restored, its Seafile kept) is taken, never uploaded twice. Turned off, the drive gives every original back before it is forgotten.

## Pages

The suite's knowledge base (`pages/`), in its own web app (`web/pages`) and its own Android app (`mobile/pages`).

- **One record type:** every page and every block in it is a `block` record (`pages/models/blocks.ts`). `parentId` is its container (a page, a column, the item it is nested under, a table for its rows), null for a top-level page; `position` orders siblings (`placeAt`). A sub-page is a `page` block in its parent's content, so the navigation's tree and the content are one structure and a move is one write. Kind-specific fields are written only when a block uses them.
- **Rich text:** a text block's `text` is one markup string (`core/services/richText.ts`: runs such as `{b,fg=red:mots}` (keys are the mark `k`), mentions `{@note=ID}`, `{@link=ID}` (Carnet's), `{@page=ID}`, dates `{@date=2026-10-28}` or `{@date=2026-10-28T1430}`), so it merges word by word as typed text (`MERGED_TEXT_FIELDS.block`, saved with `saveTypedText('block', …)`). The parser never throws: a merge may cut a run, and a date that is no real day is its characters. A mention and a date are one character in every offset; a date is shown relative to today (`core/services/dates.ts`) and written as its day wherever words must stay true (a search, a copy, Claude).
- **Table of contents:** a `toc` block holds nothing: it lists the page's headings as they are (`pageHeadings`, into columns and toggles, never into a sub-page).
- **Page actions** (the web's `web/pages`, the phone's `mobile/pages`): each is one `writeLocal` batch recorded in the page's history (`pageHistory.ts`): undo and redo write the other values with a newer clock, and typing joins a burst under 1.5 s. Blocks dropped beside another make a `columns` block of `column`s (each with a `ratio`); a set left with one column dissolves (`tidyColumns`).
- **Tables:** a `table` block holds its typed `columns` (an unknown type key reads as text) and `row` children whose `cells` are keyed by column id; a type change converts the cells (`convertCell`, `pages/services/table.ts`).
- **Captions:** a code block, a table, a media, a bookmark or an embed (`CAPTION_KINDS`) may carry a `caption`, one line of plain text merged as typed text (`saveBlockText(…, 'caption')`). A block turned into a kind that takes none loses it.
- **Mentions, bookmarks, embeds** point at a note (a collection is a note of kind `links`), a link or a page by id and show it as it is now; a deleted one reads as gone. Notes, collections and links open in a new tab, pages in place. A text holding one mention and nothing else, a bookmark and an embed switch between the three in place from the grip (`shownTarget`, `showTargetAs`); a page is never offered as an embed.
- **An embedded collection** shows as a list or a table (`view`). The table's columns are its links' fields and the facts Claude read (`pages/services/collectionTable.ts`); each embed keeps their order and which are shown (`viewColumns`). No column is added, and a row is a link added to the collection. It shows the links to do; with `showDone`, those done follow under « Déjà faits », as on the collection's screen.
- **Files:** a picture goes through the photo route, which makes its display copy; a video, a PDF or any other file through `PUT /api/files/:id`, kept as sent (100 MB at most; served as `application/octet-stream` unless it is an image, a video, audio, a PDF or plain text). The browser keeps its copy until the server has it, as for photos, and `uploaded` tells other devices when to show it. The blob collector keeps every file a block points at.
- **Bin:** a page in the bin (`trashedAt`) takes everything under it and reads as it is. After 7 days the server tombstones it and every block under it, except a sub-page put in the bin on its own, which waits for its own week.
- **A tree two devices changed at once** (`server/src/apps/pages/tree.ts`): each move is one block's `parentId`, merged alone, so two devices can make what neither did. A loop (one moved page A into B as the other moved B into A) is shown broken at its newest move (`shownParent` in `pages/services/blocks.ts`, both clients' indexes), never as in the bin; the server repairs it after the push that closed it, undoing that move (back where the push says the block came from), else sending a page of the loop to the top level. A block left in a container another device deleted (moved into a toggle as it was deleted, typed into a column a move emptied and tidied away) goes back where the push says it came from, else where the container stood; what lived in a page deleted for good stays with it, but a page in it goes to the top level. The repair is the server's write (its clock after every other), so every device takes the same tree; an hourly chore repairs what was left before.
- **Per browser, never synced** (`pagesUi.ts`, localStorage): the tree's open branches, the toggles open, the last page read.
- **Questions to Claude** (`pages/services/pagesAsk.ts`, `pages/sync/pagesAsk.ts`, `POST /api/pages/ask`, in Pages' server module): off until the Administration turns them on (`ServerSettings.pages`: `enabled`, `everywhere`, `web`, `reach`). The device builds what a question reads (`askContext`): the page, the page and its whole tree of sub-pages, or every page ranked by the search engine against the question (the page asked from first, then the hits, then the most recently changed), each page as its blocks in Markdown (a table a piece per row, embeds and bookmarks as what they show, a sub-page named), within `PAGES_REACH_CHARS[reach]`; a page that does not fit is read in part and the rest left out, and the server refuses more. A page with `private` (synced on the block, set from its menu) and everything under it is never read, nor a page in the bin. The server sends each page as a `search_result` (source `carnet-page:<id>`, title its path, one text block per block) with citations on and the pages cached (`cache_control` on the last), the earlier turns as plain messages after it, the web tool only when asked and allowed; Claude's `search_result_location` citations come back as the blocks they span (`pagesAnswer`), the web's as sources. Labels sent to Claude are fixed English; nothing is kept, the same question at once is joined, each run is billed under task `pages`, and a run cut off is billed as the app foresaw it (`askCost`). Each app holds the conversations in memory, one per page (`pagesAsk.ts`); a source opens its page at the block (the web's `#block-<id>`, the phone's `block` route parameter), and an answer laid in a page is a toggle titled with the question, the cited pages as mentions under it (`answerDraft`).
- **Phone:** Pages' own APK, with the same records and gestures: its library indexes blocks as the web's does (`blocks`, `blocksByParent`), beside Carnet's records it shows, and its page actions and history mirror the web's (change them together). Text is typed in native fields that draw the runs; an edit is read against the selection it was made on and mapped back onto the runs (`core/services/richDisplay.ts`), a mention shown as its name and taken whole. Pictures go through the photo route, other files through `PUT /api/files/:id` (sent by the sync loop); an uploaded video, PDF or file is opened from the web for now (the server serves files with the token in a header only). Its device-only state (open branches, open toggles, the last page) is a preference (`pagesUi.ts`).

## Contacts

The suite's address book (`contacts/`), in its own web app (`web/contacts`) and its own Android app (`mobile/contacts`).

- **Two record types** (`contacts/sync/types.ts`): a `contact` and a `contactLabel` (Google's « libellés »). A contact's names, company and job are plain fields; its phone numbers, e-mails and postal addresses are each one field holding a list (each entry with an id, a kind such as `mobile` or `work`, and an optional label of the person's own), written whole: two devices changing two numbers of one person at once keep the later list, as Google does. Its note is typed text, merged word by word (its type's `merged` fields). A birthday is stored as vCard writes it, `1990-03-12` or `--03-12` without the year; a 29 February falls on the 28th in other years (`services/birthdays.ts`). Favourites are a field (`favorite`, Google's « starred »). A photo is a blob (`photoId`, `photoUploaded` once the server has it), uploaded through the photo route as Carnet's are.
- **Numbers** are kept as typed and read as digits wherever it matters (`services/phones.ts`): to call (`tel:`), to compare (the last nine digits, so `06 12 34 56 78` and `+33 6 12 34 56 78` are one number), and to open WhatsApp (`https://wa.me/<international digits>`: a national number takes the calling code of the device's region, as the phone's dialer does).
- **Import** (`services/importing.ts`): Google's vCard and CSV exports (both of Google's CSV layouts, Outlook's too) and, on Android, the phone's own contacts are read into one shape, then planned against the address book: someone already there (the same e-mail, the same number, or the same name) only gains what they lacked, never loses or changes a field; a person twice in one file is one contact; label names are matched to labels, new ones made in the palette's turn; Google's « starred » becomes a favourite and its system groups are left out. The plan is written as one batch; photos come after it: bytes are uploaded as a picked photo is, an address (Google's export names each photo by its address) is fetched by the server (`POST /api/contacts/photos`, through the suite's guarded reader) and kept as an uploaded photo.
- **vCard** (`services/vcard.ts`): read from 2.1 to 4.0 (folded lines, quoted-printable, `itemN.X-ABLabel` labels, embedded or linked photos, Apple's year 1604) and written as 3.0, the version every phone and Google read. An export, a contact shared from a phone and the backups (`Contacts/Contacts.vcf`) are all vCards.
- **Mentions**: a contact is a reference type (`contact`), so a page or a formatted Carnet note mentions a person as a page mentions a note (`{@contact=<id>}`); Pages' and Carnet's clients sync Contacts' types for it (`reads`). Carnet's package reads no other app: the program names mentions (`notes/services/noteMentions.ts`, `setMentionNamer`, at start on the clients, from the records while the server writes a backup), and `bodyText`, `bodyMarkdown` and the cards' paragraphs read names through it.
- **« Mentionné dans »** (`core/sync/mentions.ts`): each app's server module answers what of its own mentions a reference (`GET /api/pages/mentions`, `GET /api/notes/mentions`, several ids of one type: a contact and those merged into it), so Contacts lists the pages and notes without syncing them; Carnet's adds, for a person, the notes and links that name them among their people (`linked`: a gift idea).
- **Duplicates** (`services/duplicates.ts`): contacts with the same e-mail, number or full name are grouped; a merge is one batch, the one kept completed as an import completes, the others binned with `mergedInto` set, and whatever resolves a contact by id (a mention, a link into Contacts) follows it (`resolveContact`).
- **People on Carnet's notes and links** (`contactIds` on a `note` and a `link`, `notes/sync/types.ts`): the contacts a note or a link is for or about, written whole as labels are. Carnet's clients name them from the Contacts records they sync (`noteMentions.peopleNames`, so the search finds them), and Carnet's module lists them where a contact is mentioned (`GET /api/notes/mentions` answers those notes and links too, said `linked`).
- **Gift ideas** (`core/sync/gifts.ts`, `server/src/apps/notes/gifts.ts`, `notes/services/gifts.ts`): Contacts (and Agenda, on a birthday) asks Carnet's module (`POST /api/notes/gifts`) to keep a product's link in its « Cadeaux » collection: the one it made (its id in `meta`), else one named so in either language, else a new one at the top of the board. The request names the contact (`contactId`): the link's people are set to it, and an address the collection holds already gains it. A request without one (an older client) writes « Pour <name> » in the comment instead. A person's ideas are the collection's links naming them (`giftIdeasFor`, an older link by its comment). Contacts syncs no Carnet record for this.
- **Birthday notifications** (Android): armed with Notifee from `birthdayAlerts` (the hour and the days before chosen in Réglages), again after each change of the library and by the reminders' refresh with the app closed (see *Notifications on time* below); exact alarms, and one whose hour went by unarmed comes late, that same day (`since`).
- **Maps** (`core/services/maps.ts`, shared with Agenda's places): on the web an address opens in the maps chosen in Réglages (`mapsUrl`: OpenStreetMap by default). On Android it opens through the suite's `openPlace` (`mobile/shared/src/app/openPlace.ts`): in the maps app chosen (« Ouvrir les adresses dans », each app's device setting, `mapsStore`), else a sheet lists the phone's maps apps itself (the `MapApps` module: the apps that open `geo:`, with their names and icons, seen through each manifest's `<queries>`), since Android handed `geo:` goes straight to its default one. The way there goes to Google Maps' and Waze's own routes, any other app gets the place.

## Agenda

The suite's calendar (`calendar/`), in its own web app (`web/calendar`, at `/agenda`) and its own Android app (`mobile/calendar`).

- **Three record types** (`calendar/sync/types.ts`): an event (`calendarEvent`), a label (`calendarLabel`, a name and a pigment, as Contacts' labels) and a calendar one subscribes to (`calendarFeed`: an iCalendar address, or the public holidays). An event's title and description are typed text (merged word by word); its description is rich text with mentions (`{@page=…}`, `{@note=…}`, `{@link=…}`, `{@contact=…}`, `{@event=…}`); its guests are contacts (`contactIds`), its files blobs (`files`, uploaded as Pages' files are).
- **When:** as iCalendar writes it, a wall time and a zone (`start` `2026-11-02T14:30`, `timeZone` `Europe/Paris`, an IANA name: the device's own when the event is made), or days for an all-day event (`2026-11-02`, its `end` the day after its last, iCalendar's DTEND). Days and wall times are counted on the civil calendar (`calendar/services/time.ts`, no daylight saving in the arithmetic); an instant is reached only through a zone, read from the platform's time zone data (`Intl`, on Hermes, the browsers and Node). The views draw every event on the device's clock: a meeting set in New York shows at its hour here, a birthday on its day.
- **Repeats** (`services/recurrence.ts`, `occurrences.ts`): RFC 5545's RRULE as text (`FREQ=MONTHLY;BYDAY=-1FR`), expanded on the device into the occurrences a view needs (DAILY to YEARLY, INTERVAL, COUNT, UNTIL, BYDAY with ordinals, BYMONTHDAY, BYMONTH, BYSETPOS, WKST); the days taken out are `exdates`, merged as a set (two devices each taking a day out keep both out); an occurrence changed alone is an exception, a record of its own (`recurringId`, `originalStart`), its id derived from the series and the occurrence (`exceptionId`, a UUID of version 8), so two devices changing one occurrence write one record whose fields merge. Exceptions made for one occurrence before ids were derived show once (the later edited, `exceptionAt`), and deleting it takes them all. Changes follow Google's three choices (`services/editing.ts`, the same batches on both clients): « Cet événement » makes or changes the exception, « Cet événement et les suivants » ends the series the day before (UNTIL) and starts a new one with the days taken out and the exceptions after it (its id derived too, `followingId`: two devices splitting at the same day make one series; split at two different days, both new series show, overlapping, until one is deleted, as nothing tells the halves of a double split from two events), « Tous les événements » changes the series and moves its exceptions' places along with its hour.
- **Views** (`services/layout.ts`): a day's timed events side by side where they overlap (a cluster shares the width, each in its first free column, widened over free columns), all-day and multi-day bars in lanes across a week, « +2 » for what does not fit; day, three days, week, month, year and a schedule.
- **Subscriptions and holidays are records**: the server reads each subscribed address (on adding it, every 6 hours, on demand) and the public holidays (Nager.Date, for the country of `ServerSettings.calendar.holidays`, last year to two years ahead, the `holidays` calendar of fixed id), and keeps their events as Agenda's own records, marked with their calendar (`feedId`) and their UID; each reading writes only what changed (`services/feeds.ts`, `planFeed`), so the sync carries nothing when nothing moved and every device has them offline, its widgets and search too. They are read-only in the apps; « Dupliquer » makes one's own copy. A calendar is hidden on every device (`hidden`), or deleted with its events.
- **iCalendar** (`services/ics.ts`): Google Agenda's export (Takeout), a subscribed address, an `.ics` opened or shared on the phone are read (zones by IANA name, Windows' names, or a VTIMEZONE's location; DURATION, EXDATE, RECURRENCE-ID, cancelled occurrences, VALARM); an import (`services/importing.ts`) adds each UID once (one in the bin comes again), as one batch, taken back whole on its slip (tombstones: `forgetImport`) or later from the same file (its events of one's own to the bin, with their exceptions: `importedEvents`, `trashImported`). The published calendar, the backups' `Agenda.ics` and an export are written as one VEVENT per event or exception, times in UTC unless they repeat (then in their zone, as Google writes them). The published calendar, which anyone holding its address reads, names a description's mentions of contacts and of the events it publishes only, every other one « … » (`publishedNamer`, `server/src/apps/calendar/names.ts`).
- **From the other apps** (layers each device shows or hides, `CalendarPrefs`): contacts' birthdays (`services/layers.ts`, with WhatsApp, a message, a call and the gift ideas of Carnet's « Cadeaux » collection a tap away), Carnet's notes with a reminder, the days written in pages (`pages/services/pageDates.ts`, never a private page's). Agenda's clients sync Contacts', Carnet's and Pages' records for these and to name what a description mentions.
- **Mentions both ways**: Pages and Carnet's formatted notes mention events (their clients sync Agenda's types); « Mentionné dans » on an event lists the pages and notes that mention it (their modules' `mentions` routes), and a page, a note or a contact lists the events that mention it or have the person as a guest (`GET /api/calendar/mentions`, `eventsMentioning`).
- **Reminders**: minutes before the start (an all-day event's from its day's midnight). The phone arms them with Notifee from `alarmsBetween` (the nearest 64, thirty days ahead, the hourly refresh arming the next); the web gets them as Web Push notifications the server sends (VAPID, encrypted per RFC 8291 with `node:crypto`), so a closed tab still rings. A reminder's moment is always a whole minute, so the server ticks on the clock's minutes, 1.5 s early for the push service's own delay (`nextTickIn`), with `Urgency: high`, a TTL until the reminder stops being worth it (`pushTtl`) and a `Topic` per reminder, each browser told side by side. Both sides ring late, once, what went by unrung while still worth it (`stillWorthRinging`: within the hour, and before its event is ten minutes under way): the server after a stop, the phone after an event synced late or an alarm that failed.
- **Claude** (with a key): an event typed in words the device could not read (`services/quickAdd.ts` reads French and English first: days, spans, hours, lengths, places), an event drawn from a saved link (Carnet's « Ajouter à l'agenda » module), and questions about the calendar, off until the Administration turns them on, built on the device (`services/ask.ts`: fixed English labels around the person's words, the nearest occurrences first). Nothing is written before the person saves the form.
- **The weather** of a day with a placed event (Open-Meteo, through the server, 16 days ahead).

## Search

- **The spotlight** (web: ⌘P or Ctrl+P, and Ctrl+K): an in-memory full-text index in core (`core/services/searchIndex.ts`) over the app's own documents, each app making its own (`notes/services/spotlight.ts`: a note's lines and checklist items, a link's reading and comment, labels, sites, kinds and collections as meta; `pages/services/spotlight.ts`: a page's blocks), keyed by `core/services/spotlight.ts`. A new text worth finding is added there; a new app adds its documents.
- **Matching:** words folded as everywhere (`foldText`), found whole, by their start, in the plural, inside a longer word (four letters or more) and by a title's initials; with one typo from four letters (two from eight) only when the words as typed find fewer than eight hits. A word of one or two letters finds titles by its start, the content only whole. Every word is required but the small ones (« de », "the"); `"…"` keeps words together, `-word` leaves out.
- **Ranking:** the titles that answer alone first, then the content (BM25F over title, headings, body and meta, the best few weighed again for how close their words sit, a little for a recent change), then, when nothing holds every word, what holds the most, saying what it lacks. What this browser opened lately, or picked for the same query, lifts a result within its tier (localStorage, never synced).
- **Index:** built again after each change of the library, a document whose words did not change keeping its entry, and once while the browser is idle after the start. The board's search box (`/`) and the phone keep their simpler matching (`core/services/search.ts`, `pageSearch.ts`).

## Interface language

French or English, chosen per device in Réglages (`locale` in each client's settings). `core/i18n` holds the language and `t()`: the French sentence is the key, the English comes from the dictionaries each program adds at start (core's in `core/i18n/en/`, the design system's in `design/i18n/en.ts`, Carnet's in `notes/i18n/en/`, one part per area), and a sentence without English shows in French. `tools/i18n/check.mjs` checks that each package's sentences have their English in a dictionary that package can count on, so an app installed alone never lacks another app's. The formatting helpers (`core/services/format.ts`: dates, numbers, prices, `plural`) follow it. A new language draws every screen again where it was: a web app remounts on the same address (`LocaleRoot`), the phone remounts its navigation with the stack it had. Until one is chosen, a device takes its own system's language (French when it is French, English otherwise). The server never sets a language for its own words: its messages stay French and the apps translate the fixed ones they show (`en/server.ts` in core's dictionary and in Carnet's). What Claude writes follows the link: a link carries `lang`, the interface language of the device that saved it (set again when it is read again), and the server names it on the first line of its message to Claude; a cached reading is reused only in the same language. Android's own strings (widget picker descriptions) follow the system.

## Phone specifics

Carnet's APK below; Pages' shares everything but the widgets and the reminders; Contacts' and Agenda's after it.

- **Board:** a masonry wall of paper sheets: each card is exactly as tall as what it shows, computed from its anatomy and Signika's advance widths, never measured (`notes/services/wall.ts`, `textWrap.ts`), so the wall is virtualised without a layout pass and the text is cut to the lines that were counted.
- **Native modules** (every spec in `mobile/shared/specs/`, which each app's codegen reads; the shared Kotlin in `mobile/shared/android/` (media, haptics, connectivity, the organic view, the suite link, the launcher's icon, headless tasks, the background sync; the reminders' module in `src/reminders`, compiled only into the apps that ring), Carnet's own in `mobile/notes/android/.../intake`, `.../widget`): the share target (text and images, copied into app storage before the grant lapses), the photo picker and camera (images re-encoded upright, at most 2560 px), the Keep Takeout zip (only `Keep/` entries, zip-slip safe), and the system's own haptic clicks for gesture thresholds and long presses (no vibration permission).
- **Nothing in Android's backups** (every app): `allowBackup="false"`, and `res/xml/data_extraction_rules.xml` (Android 12 and later: cloud backups and the transfer to a new phone) with `backup_rules.xml` (older) excluding every domain, since the app's storage holds the token and every record; a new phone connects again and syncs. Notifee's `NotificationReceiverActivity`, which a notification's tap goes through, is overridden as not exported in the apps that ring (`tools:replace`): only their own PendingIntents reach it.
- **The launcher's icon** (every app): its mark's pebble in the app's primary, as in the app (`markColors`). Android cannot paint a launcher icon, so each app ships its icon in every palette's primary (`PALETTES`), each the icon of a launcher entry of its own: an `<activity-alias>` of MainActivity, `.LauncherCarnet` to `.LauncherMousse`, its meta-data naming its palette, Carnet's on as installed (all generated by `tools/icons/build_android_drawables.mjs`, the entries between marks in each manifest; MainActivity keeps every other intent filter and has no LAUNCHER one). The app turns on the entry of `launcherPalette(primary)` (the palette's own, or the palette whose primary is nearest in OKLab to one picked precisely) and the others off (`LauncherIcon` module, `DONT_KILL_APP`, all at once from Android 13, the new one on first before). Turning an entry off closes the task the launcher opened through it and may drop its shortcut from the home screen (Launcher3), so the switch waits until the app leaves the screen and happens only when the palette changes (`mobile/shared/src/app/launcherIcon.ts`), never at start, nor while another activity (a picker, the camera, a permission's dialog) sits on MainActivity in that task, since React Native calls a paused app backgrounded and closing the task would lose what it hands back. With Android 13's themed icons on, the launcher draws the monochrome layer in the wallpaper's colours instead, whatever the palette. Never rename or remove an entry: a phone that turned it on would lose the app from its launcher. What names the launcher's activity must name MainActivity instead: a notification's tap (`launchActivity()`, since Notifee's `'default'` loads the launcher's activity as a class and an alias is not one), `npm run android` (`--main-activity .MainActivity`); widgets and links already open MainActivity by its class or its scheme. The widgets, the share sheet's targets, the app's own icon in Android's settings and the web's install icons keep Carnet's matcha; the web's tab icon follows the primary exactly.
- **What the apps take from Android** (every app unless said; each arrives through the app's `IntakeModule`, parked until JavaScript takes it once setup is done, `launch/IntentExtras.kt`):
  - *The share sheet:* Carnet takes text, links and pictures, and puts its collections in the sheet's first row: plain dynamic sharing shortcuts in `com.stephenprn.carnet.category.COLLECTION` (`res/xml/shortcuts.xml`'s `<share-target>`), the four a link was last filed into, each its emblem on its stone, published by `ShortcutsModule` from `src/app/shareShortcuts.ts` at start, when collections change and after a launcher icon switch; a share through one files its links there as the person's (`filedBy: 'person'`). Pages takes text as a new page; Contacts a vCard; Agenda an iCalendar file.
  - *Selected text* (`PROCESS_TEXT`): « Note in Carnet » (a link is filed, words become a note), « Create event » (Agenda's quick add, filled in).
  - *Another app's forms:* `ACTION_INSERT` and `INSERT_OR_EDIT` for a contact (ContactsContract's extras and `data` rows, `handedContact.ts`) open Contacts' new contact filled in; `ACTION_INSERT` for an event (CalendarContract's extras, `handedEvent.ts`: instants into wall times in the event's zone or the phone's, an all-day event's days from UTC midnight, an RRULE the calendar package reads) opens Agenda's new event filled in, its guests matched to contacts by e-mail and the others on the description's last line. Nothing is saved until the person does.
  - *Links:* `webcal:` and `webcals:` open Agenda's « Other calendars » with the https address, to subscribe; `content://com.android.calendar/time/<ms>` (the Clock, other apps' « open the calendar ») opens that day.
  - *Launcher shortcuts* (`res/xml/shortcuts.xml`; each launcher alias's `android.app.shortcuts`, written by `tools/icons/build_android_drawables.mjs` with the icons, Onde's glyphs on the matcha tile): Carnet's note, list, link and search; Pages' page and search; Contacts' new contact, favourites, birthdays and search; Agenda's new event, quick add, today and search. They land on `ShortcutActivity`, which hands the link to MainActivity: Android clears the task a static shortcut opens, which would close what is open.
  - *Quick Settings tiles:* Carnet's « New note », Agenda's « New event » (`tile/`), opening once the phone is unlocked.
  - The words Android shows for these (the share sheet's, the selection menu's, shortcuts', tiles') are Android strings, English in `values/` and French in `values-fr/`, read in the phone's language.
- **Sync with the app closed** (every app; `com.stephenprn.unlease.backgroundsync`, spec `NativeBackgroundSync.ts`, `mobile/shared/src/app/sync/backgroundTask.ts`): the sync loop runs only in the foreground (a long-poll held in the background keeps the radio awake); WorkManager runs the app's `syncTask` headless (`HeadlessTasks.kt`) whenever a network is there: every 15 minutes while a server is set, soon after the app leaves with changes or files unsent (`pauseSync`) or after a write made with the app closed (a widget's tick, a notification's answer), retried with a doubling wait from 30 s until JavaScript reports `done` (nothing left to send; a refused token is not retried), and 5 s after any network comes up while changes wait or the last sync is over 10 minutes old (`SyncReceiver`, through a `PendingIntent` network watch the system keeps with the app closed). Android's default network request leaves VPNs out (`NET_CAPABILITY_NOT_VPN`); the watch removes it, so a server reached only through a VPN gets the changes as soon as the VPN comes up. The watch is lost at a restart or an update and registered again then (`BOOT_COMPLETED`, `MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED`). The task makes one exchange (`syncInBackground`, unless the foreground loop runs), uploads the files, then does the app's own (Carnet's, Contacts' and Agenda's notifications armed, Agenda's widgets). A battery set to « Restreinte », Data Saver on mobile data, a maker's battery saver, or a force stop holds it until the app is opened again. The person is told (`useBackgroundHealth`, `health` and `open` in the module): while a server is set, Réglages shows « Synchronisation en arrière-plan » with a row for the battery's « Restreinte » (`isBackgroundRestricted`) and for Data Saver (`getRestrictBackgroundStatus`), each opening the page that fixes it and saying what to choose there, the same rows sit in the Administration's « Serveur », and a slip says it at start or back in front, once a day at most, its « Corriger » opening that page. In an app that rings, that battery row speaks for the notifications too, and « Notifications à l'heure » leaves its own out.
- **Reminders** are armed on the phone with Notifee from the synced `reminderAt` (`mobile/notes/src/app/hooks/useReminders.ts`); one whose moment went by before it reached the phone rings late within the day. With the app closed, the background sync and the reminders' refresh pull and arm (above, below). An answer from the notification's buttons is sent at once (`flushNow`).
- **Notifications on time** (Carnet's reminders and kitchen timers, Contacts' birthdays, Agenda's events): the research behind it is in each piece's comments; in short:
  - *Exact alarms.* Android 14 refuses `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` by default, and an inexact alarm may come up to an hour late, so the three apps hold `USE_EXACT_ALARM` (Android 13+, granted at install, never taken back; Play keeps it to calendars and alarm clocks, and the APKs are sideloaded) with `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` up to Android 12. Alarms are `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle` (72 an hour in Doze), a kitchen timer `setAlarmClock` (never held back, not even by a battery set to « Restreinte »). A notification says its category (event, reminder, alarm), which « Ne pas déranger » reads.
  - *One book per app* (`mobile/shared/src/app/alarms.ts`, `planAlarms`): what is wanted now against what this phone armed, by id. An alarm that fails to arm (Notifee refuses a moment already past) is not remembered, so it is tried again; one whose moment went by unarmed rings at once (`displayNotification`) while still worth it, never twice, and not on a book's first run (it does not know what rang). Everything is armed again when the mode changes (exact allowed, or an older build's inexact alarms), when a build arms them another way (`ARMED_BY`: since the launcher's icon follows the primary, a tap names MainActivity by its class, see below), or after a force stop, which takes every alarm away (`stoppedBefore`: Android 15's start reason, Android 11's last exit at the person's request).
  - *The refresh with the app closed* (`com.stephenprn.unlease.reminders`, spec `NativeReminders.ts`): WorkManager runs the app's `remindersTask` headless (`HeadlessTasks.kt`) every hour (Carnet's while a note has a reminder, Contacts' twice a day while birthdays are on; `keepFresh`), and soon after a restart, an update, the clock or the zone set, the language changed, exact alarms allowed (`RefreshReceiver`, protected broadcasts). The task pulls once (`pullNow`, unless the foreground loop runs) and reconciles. Notifee arms its own triggers again after a restart.
  - *What Android allows* (`health`): notifications, exact alarms, the battery's « Restreinte » (`isBackgroundRestricted`), the battery optimization. Réglages' « Notifications à l'heure » (`ReminderHealthSection`) shows a row only for what is wrong, and a maker's battery saver (Notifee's `getPowerManagerInfo`) only on phones that have one, with Android's « Sans restriction » question beside it.
- **Widgets** (`mobile/notes/android/.../widget`): Actions rapides and Classer un lien hold no data; Collection, Notes d'un label and Note draw what JavaScript hands them. `mobile/notes/src/app/widgetPayloads.ts` builds each placed widget's picture as JSON, with the app's wording and order; `mobile/notes/src/app/widgets.ts`, started by `bootstrap` wherever JavaScript runs, sends it again 400 ms after the library changes, through the `Widgets` module. The native side only draws (RemoteViews) and routes taps: `carnet://` links into the app, a website through `WidgetRouterActivity`. A list's rows share one mutable template, which the launcher holding it may fill in with anything, so what it fills in is checked where it lands: the router opens only a `carnet://` page or a website, and a tick only an item the widget shows (`WidgetTickReceiver`: the widget's id rides in the template's extras, which a fill-in cannot replace). The receiver and the router are not exported.
  - **Ticks** on the Note widget show at once, wait in the native store, and are written by JavaScript as one change (`setChecks`): in a headless task started from the broadcast (`WidgetTasks.kt`, `widgetTask.ts`; a closed app may not start a service), or at the next start.
  - **The quick window** (`QuickActivity`, the `quickWindow` root): the link field and the pickers, drawn by the app over the home screen, its window the scrim; also the launcher's configuration step when a widget is placed.
  - **Look:** colours, continuous-corner sheets (corner vectors and bands, as a launcher cannot clip), pebbles, emblems and glyphs come from `design` through `tools/widgets/build_widget_resources.mjs`. A widget is a slab of the ground: its two tones down the middle band, under the web's film grain (`tools/textures/build_grain.py`, both themes); its sheets carry the paper's speckle at the theme's strength. The grain is laid in pieces that step along each corner's curve, since a tile cannot be clipped. A launcher cannot load an app's font: titles are set in Signika by the app as tinted images (`WidgetType.kt`), the rest is the system font. Link pictures are fetched with the device's token and cut to their frame (`Thumbnails.kt`).

**Contacts' APK** (`mobile/contacts`) shares Carnet's frame, without its board, its reminders or its link intake:

## Deployment

One image (root `Dockerfile`, published by `.github/workflows/image.yml` as `ghcr.io/stephenprn/unlease` for amd64 and arm64) builds the server and the web apps; the server serves each web app under its path (`/notes`, `/pages`, `/` sending to the first app it runs, v0.1's addresses moved to their place) and the API at `/api` on one port (`server/src/http/web.ts`, `UNLEASE_WEB_DIR`), so the apps and their API share an origin and need no CORS. A web app recognises a server serving it (`/api/health` on its own origin) and fills in its address. The same builds also work from any static host (`_headers`, `_redirects` at the root), then with `UNLEASE_CORS_ORIGINS`. `docker-compose.yml` and `docs/SELF-HOSTING.md` are the reference deployment; each app's APK is attached to each release by `.github/workflows/android.yml`.

**Why one image for every app**, rather than one per app: the apps share one Node process, one SQLite database, one sync and one set of settings, and the image is small. Suites split their images for another runtime or for heavy, optional hardware ([Immich keeps machine learning apart](https://docs.immich.app/developer/architecture): Python, a GPU, possibly another machine), or to scale parts apart; for a household's server that is more to run and upgrade for nothing. Mealie went from two images to one ([its installation checklist](https://docs.mealie.io/documentation/getting-started/installation/installation-checklist)); Plane keeps several and calls its all-in-one image an evaluation ([Plane AIO](https://developers.plane.so/self-hosting/methods/docker-aio)) because its services scale apart, which ours do not. Docker's own guidance is one service per container, a service possibly several processes ([Docker](https://docs.docker.com/engine/containers/multi-service_container/)): the suite's server is one service. Apps are turned on and off at runtime instead, as Nextcloud's are ([its apps management](https://docs.nextcloud.com/server/stable/admin_manual/apps_management.html)); a part that ever needs another runtime or a GPU gets an image of its own.

**Why a web app per app, on one origin**: each app is installed, opened and worked in on its own, as its APK is, with its own page, icon, manifest, service worker and IndexedDB database (its slug: `carnet`, `pages`); on one origin, under its own path (`/notes`, `/pages`), they share the device's settings (the server, the colours, the language: `unlease.settings`), so the second app asks nothing, and a reference from one into the other is a plain address of the other ([web.dev on several PWAs on one domain](https://web.dev/articles/building-multiple-pwas-on-the-same-domain): each its own scope and manifest). A link into the other app loads its page whole; nothing else crosses (each syncs on its own, Pages with Carnet's records it shows).

**Why an APK per app**: each app is installed only where it is wanted and has its own launcher icon, widgets and system integrations (a calendar or contacts app will register with Android's providers); they share `mobile/shared` and, on the phone, one connection: the second app installed reads the first one's (address, token, colours) through a provider guarded by a signature permission, so only the suite's own apps, signed with the same key, can read it; and the reader reads a sibling's provider only once Android names that sibling's package as its owner and finds it signed with its own key (`SuiteLinkModule`), so an app that took a sibling's authority, or the permission before the suite was installed, hands over no connection of its own. The same provider carries the grains and the language, which are the suite's (the latest choice wins), and a look one app gives others (« Appliquer aux autres »: each app keeps its own until then).

**The setup** follows what makes first runs easy elsewhere ([Home Assistant's onboarding](https://www.home-assistant.io/getting-started/onboarding): few steps, a backup to restore offered first, every field explained, [its rule for setup forms](https://contributors.home-assistant.io/core/quality-scale/config-flow/)): one decision per step, every external service optional and left for later, each field saying where its value is found, nothing asked twice (the address known when served, Claude's key once for every app, a phone's second app reusing the first one's connection, a browser's second web app the device's settings).

## Web specifics

- **Storage:** an app's records in its own IndexedDB database (`carnet`, Carnet's name since v0.1, and `pages`), written behind memory a tick later (a failed write is retried); an app's tabs share its database and post what they write, pending marks and bases included, on a BroadcastChannel. The device's settings are every app's (`unlease.settings`, followed across tabs; v0.1's `carnet.settings` read until they exist).
- **Offline:** each app's service worker (`web/public/sw.js`, production builds, scoped to its path: `/notes`, allowed by `Service-Worker-Allowed`) keeps its page, its hashed assets and the fonts, so the installed app opens without a network and works from IndexedDB; it never touches the API nor the other app's path. The root's `sw.js` (`web/root/sw.js`) retires v0.1's single worker.

## Deviations from the house structure

| Rule | Here | Why |
